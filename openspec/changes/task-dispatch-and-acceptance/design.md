## Context

The task is a standalone unit of assigned work. Business decomposition is a human process; the app records concrete assignments, not a parent/child task graph. A plain-text association note can preserve context without affecting authorization or lifecycle.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** enforce role-bounded assignment (company-wide for Founder; self, same-department Team Leaders, or other-department Department Managers for Department Managers; team-scoped for Team Leaders; self-only for Employees), one primary and optional secondary assignees, direct-supervisor approval, clear creator/assignee ownership, planned start/deadline fields, text-only execution history, separate completion acceptance, auditability, read-only hierarchical visibility, and efficient task-list filtering.

**Non-Goals:** task trees, a split-task operation, automatic or percentage progress, recipient acceptance, attachment uploads, general-purpose task search outside subordinate inspection, or cross-department workflow details in this change.

## Decisions

- A task has one creator, exactly one primary assignee, and zero or more secondary assignees. The role eligibility matrix applies to both groups: Founder may choose any enabled company member; Department Manager may choose themself, a Team Leader in their department, or another Department Manager; Team Leader may choose themself or an Employee in their team; Employee may choose themself only. Secondary assignees must be enabled, unique, within the same candidate scope, and distinct from the primary. Founder selectors show a searchable organization tree: Founder and enabled departments are parallel roots, department/team nodes expand or collapse, and name/role search shows matching eligible members; clearing search restores the tree. Other roles use searchable flat lists. Candidate text and selection state need readable foreground/background contrast. Department Manager assignments to another department's manager use the unified entry and are detailed in the companion change.
- Keep optional `association_note` as ordinary text. Do not add `parent_task_id`, task-tree traversal, roll-up, inherited permissions, or cascade behavior.
- Keep `estimated_start_at` as an optional planned schedule field. It must be no later than required `due_at`; it does not record actual work start. Task creation/edit uses year-month-day and hour-minute wheel selectors. Task-list estimated-start and deadline filters use year-month-day wheels and remain date-only. When either estimated-start boundary is set, tasks without an estimated start do not match.
- Keep task completion status binary (`unfinished` / `completed`). Approval, returned-for-editing, waiting-for-acceptance, and cancellation are separate workflow/audit records, not additional completion states.
- Use a separate approval record and queues. If the creator has a direct supervisor, every creation requires that supervisor's approval, including self-assigned work. Founder is the top role and bypasses approval.
- Before approval, the creator and approver access the task only in their respective Approval Center queues; it does not appear in ordinary task lists or to any assignee. After approval, it appears in the creator's `Created by Me` and in `My Tasks` for the primary and every secondary assignee. A creator who is also assigned sees the same task record in both views.
- A returned approval requires a reason. The creator edits and resubmits. Changes to the primary or secondary assignee set, estimated start, deadline, task details, or expected deliverables after approval require approval again.
- Task-definition history records tag-association snapshots as ordered `{id, name}` values and both primary and secondary assignee snapshots as `{id, display_name}`, using names at operation time. Task history reads resolve legacy numeric-only snapshots where the referenced record remains available.
- Render task details by relationship, not as one shared status/history page: creators and the designated approver can see approval state and approval rounds; primary and secondary assignees who are neither creator nor approver see execution information without approval state/history; only the primary assignee can submit or resubmit completion; a read-only hierarchy viewer sees authorized task/execution information without approval records or actions. If a user has multiple relationships, combine the corresponding information while keeping each action subject to server authorization. Founder-exempt tasks do not show an empty approval-history section.
- Do not model a separate actual task-start state or timestamp in the MVP. Remove the start button and `started_at`; the optional planned `estimated_start_at` is not execution activity. The first actual progress update or completion submission records execution activity.
- Generate an unread approval notification for the creator's direct supervisor when a subordinate task awaits approval. After approval, generate a separate unread assignment notification for the primary and each secondary assignee; no assignee is notified before approval. Suppress the creator's own assignment notice when the creator is the primary assignee. Opening the notification list does not mark notices read; reading never changes task or approval state.
- When an approval round is decided, resolve its pending approver notice and create one unread `approval_approved` or `approval_returned` event notice for the task creator. Include the round and decision comment; preserve result notices across later resubmission. Creator result notices open the creator's task detail and remain unread until individually opened.
- Primary and secondary assignees may add append-only text progress updates, each attributed to the actual author and timestamp. Only the primary assignee may submit or resubmit completion details. Acceptance/return outcome notices go only to the primary assignee; secondary assignees do not receive those outcomes. Do not store percentage completion.
- Completion submission has its own form and record: completion explanation is required; deliverable information is required only when expected deliverables were specified at creation. MVP has no upload requirement.
- Only the direct dispatcher may accept or return submitted work. Return requires a reason. Self-assigned work is marked complete by its primary assignee without self-acceptance. Cancellation notices go to every primary or secondary assignee other than the creator. Acceptance and return notices go only to the primary assignee.
- Direct dispatcher may cancel their unfinished task with a reason. Cancellation archives only that task; there is no cascade because tasks are not physically linked.
- Task notifications follow workflow state as well as read state. Cancellation resolves outstanding approval, assignment, submission, and return notices, then sends an unread cancellation notice to the current pending approver or, for an approved assignment, each primary or secondary assignee other than the creator. Completion submission resolves assignment notices and notifies the direct dispatcher; acceptance or return notifies only the primary assignee. Return notices are resolved when the primary submits a new result. Self-assigned completion creates no self-notices. Resolved notices remain in history but are excluded from unread counts and unread-only results; resolving a notice does not mark it read.
- Supervisors may inspect descendant users' tasks read-only. Visibility does not grant approve, edit, accept, return, or cancel authority. Only the creator's direct supervisor approves; only the creator/direct dispatcher accepts.
- Ordinary task lists do not show permanent category/status tabs. One filter form handles the main list's task scope (`全部` / `我的任务` / `我创建的`), optional inclusive estimated-start and deadline date ranges, and completion state (`全部` / `未完成` / `已完成`). Date boundaries are selected with year-month-day wheels at day precision; when an estimated-start boundary is used, tasks without an estimated start do not match. `全部` is the de-duplicated union of the current user's approved primary/secondary assigned and created tasks. Defaults are `我的任务`, unrestricted date ranges, and all completion states. The subordinate read-only list has the same date/status filters, a task-scope toggle (`承担的任务` / `创建的任务`, default assigned), and a name-search target selector defaulting to all authorized descendants. In assigned mode, filter tasks where the selected person is primary or secondary assignee; in created mode, filter tasks by their creator identity. Both modes show only approved tasks; pending/returned tasks stay in the Approval Center. The all-descendants option applies the selected mode across all visible descendants. Search and object selection operate only within the server-authorized visible set. The assignment-recipient set and the subordinate-visibility set are independently validated by their existing role rules: e.g. a Department Manager may assign to another department's manager, but that peer manager is not thereby a visible subordinate.
- Render ordinary and subordinate task results in one horizontally scrollable, aligned table with a single task per row and columns for title, creator, primary assignee, estimated start, deadline, and completion status. The assignee column shows only the primary; task detail shows the secondary assignees. Show `未设置` when estimated start is absent; for cancelled tasks, retain the completion value and add a separate cancellation indicator. Row selection opens the existing role-aware detail. Keep Approval Center entries in their dedicated approval-list layout.
- Place Approval Center and subordinate-inspection shortcuts in the task-center top-right area, and expose task creation through a fixed bottom-right circular plus button on ordinary list screens. Do not show the creation FAB in approval, detail, or task-form screens. Approval queues remain separate and do not use ordinary-list filters.
- `Created by Me` replaces the former dispatched-work label. `My Tasks` is not divided into department-source categories; assignments from other Department Managers are ordinary tasks and have no separate marker or filter.

## Data and Authorization Boundaries

Keep `tasks_task.assignee_id` as the required primary assignee and represent secondary assignees in a many-to-many relation table with unique `(task_id, user_id)` pairs; a user cannot be both primary and secondary for the same task. Create/edit API payloads use `secondary_assignee_ids`, while task history snapshots each person's ID and operation-time display name. Represent task definition, approval history, text updates, completion submissions/decisions, and cancellation audit separately as needed. The service must enforce authorization on every operation; UI hiding is not a security boundary.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Approval adds a gate before assignment visibility; show the task in both creator and approver queues inside the separate Approval Center and notify both sides to make the wait explicit.
- Read-only hierarchical visibility is broader than direct assignment visibility; apply organization-scope filtering and keep workflow actions tied to creator/assignee relationships.
- Text association is less queryable than a foreign key, intentionally avoiding artificial task-tree coupling and complex cascading behavior.

## Migration Plan

This is an MVP greenfield workflow. Implement organization/supervisor lookup first, then task creation and approval, then execution and acceptance. Do not introduce task-tree migration logic. Retain audit history for all workflow decisions.
